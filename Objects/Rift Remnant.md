# Quick Reference
**Location:** A general rules note. One remnant appears in [[Prologue 1 - Drawg, Elby & Seraphina]]: a bead found in the dead dryad's remains, tied to the Feyrealm.
**Significance:** A rift remnant focuses Plane Shift and disrupts teleportation nearby. The tables below give the effect.

# Description & Properties

## Typical Appearance


- **Shape**: Rift remnants typically have an irregular, blob-like shape, reminiscent of the jagged tear that created them. Edges might be smooth in some places but sharply angular in others, as if the piece was torn from a larger, unseen whole.
- **Color and Texture**: The remnants exhibit a swirling mix of colors, each streak and hue representing a different aspect of the planes involved in the rift. These colors shift and move within the remnant, never fully settling, as if the object is still not entirely stable. The texture is glass-like, smooth and hard to the touch, but with an inner light that seems to move and pulse faintly as if it were liquid.
- **Unique Markings**: Some remnants might bear unique markings or patterns, echoes of the demiplanes or entities that were caught in the planar eddy. These could take the form of strange symbols, ghostly images, or even faint sounds or whispers emanating from within. 
- Specific physical properties can often be used to identify the plane to which it was once connected. For instance, a reddish-orange remnant may have originated in the Plane of Fire.

## Properties
A rift remnant can function as a focus for the Plane Shift spell, taking the caster to the plane it was originally connected to. Casters on another plane using the remnant to return to the Material Plane will appear within a half mile of the location where the remnant was formed.

Any teleportation magic that is cast in the presence of a rift remnant has a chance to function incorrectly. Use the [[Teleport|Teleport]] table to determine the outcome for all spells below 7th-Level. Use the "Rift Remnant Size" table to compute a Remnant Effect Size value, then use the conversion table to determine which row of the Teleport table to use:

| Rift Remnant Size | Comparison | Effect Size |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Small | Small pebble | 1 |
| Medium | Gold piece | 3 |
| Large | Fist | 6 |
| Huge | Anything larger | 12 |

| Effect Size | Teleport Table Row (below 7th level spells) | Teleport Spell Row change |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| 1 | Associated object (i.e. works correctly) | Use table as is |
| 2 | Very familiar (roll d100+15) | Use table as is |
| 3 | Very familiar (roll d100+5) | Use one row lower* (roll d100+10) |
| 4-5 | Very familiar (roll d100) | Use one row lower |
| 6-8 | Seen casually (roll d100+10) | Use two rows lower (roll d100+20) |
| 9-11 | Seen casually (roll d100) | Use two rows lower |
| 12-20 | Viewed once | Use three rows lower |
| 21-30 | Description | Use four rows lower |
| 30+ | False destination | Use five rows lower |
\*So, for example, if the caster is Very familiar with the destination, use the numbers on the "Seen casually" row.

# Lore

In the tumultuous multiverse of D&D, planar rifts are not mere openings but violent tears in the fabric of reality. These tears can occur when the delicate veil between planes is strained by powerful magic, natural cataclysms, or the meddling of gods and otherworldly beings. When such a rift is torn open, it creates a maelstrom of planar energy, a chaotic eddy where the rules of multiple realities collide and twist.

As a rift violently rips through the multiverse, it doesn't cleanly puncture from one plane to another; instead, it tears through the intricate weave of reality. This tear might inadvertently clip or pass through nearby demiplanes or weaker planar boundaries. The tear's edge is ragged, frayed with the essence of multiple realities, pulling and twisting whatever comes near.

Around this tear forms a planar eddy, a swirling vortex of intermingling energies and substances from the connected planes and any demiplanes caught in the fray. In this tumultuous zone, fragments of reality—bits of demiplanes, planar energy, and perhaps even echoes of beings or objects from various planes—are trapped and forced together. This trap is not a stable place; it's a violent, swirling storm of what might be considered planar debris.

## Creation of Rift Remnants


As the rift begins to close, either naturally or through the efforts of powerful beings, the planar eddy's chaotic spin slows, and the trapped energies and fragments begin to settle. The closure of the rift exerts a compressive force, fusing these disparate pieces into a solid form. The resulting object is a rift remnant, an amorphous glass-like pebble that embodies the chaos and mixture of the planar eddy.
