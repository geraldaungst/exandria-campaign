## General Information

- [x] Put your background info into a Google Doc and share it with the DM. This will not be shared with other players so you should include all secrets, even if you aren’t sharing them with other characters.
- [x] Exandria is what I would consider a “high-magic” world. Magic is abundant and common, and you will have fairly ready access to magic items. The story and encounters will be scaled accordingly.
- [x] Exandria is a diverse world with a diverse population. You will run into a variety of genders, races, and behaviors as well as a variety of beliefs and customs about them. We may encounter situations involving religion, politics, violence, sexuality (including sexual assault), captivity/slavery, gender roles, romance, racism, and prejudice. We will talk more about limits and boundaries a bit later.
- [x] We will also review some other important House Rules and game mechanics listed in the Campaign handouts. All of the house rules are open for discussion by the group. Feel free to make comments in the document.
- [x] **Players** are responsible for managing your individual and group supplies, ammunition, collective loot, inventories, finances, etc. **Appoint one person to be responsible for group resources**. I will not nitpick, but I may call out a situation that seems questionable, like a ranger who has gone through multiple combats without stocking up on arrows.
- [x] Don’t make any assumptions about anything without sharing the info with the DM. For example, don’t assume that you had time to scavenge for arrows after a battle unless you say your character is doing that.
- [x] A handout is available in the game for notetaking. If you would like additional journal pages either for your own individual notes or for the whole group, I’m happy to create as many as you like. Just know that the DM can see everything in all of the notes. :) 
- [x] As DM, I lean towards a RAW style of play and would prefer to look up a rule rather than improvise a call. That said, what’s your personal limit? At what point (10 seconds, 2 minutes) do you want me to just make a call and move on?
- [x] I also tend to let the dice fall where they fall and deal with the aftermath, though I will also improvise and adjust where necessary to keep the story fun.
- [x] I would like to give players room to create descriptions of scenes and environments when it makes sense to do so. For example, if we are in an area that a character would be very familiar with (hometown, a tavern where they’ve spent a lot of time, etc.) or encountering an NPC that is close to the character, I may ask the player to describe it rather than the DM. Consider how much of this you’re comfortable with.
- [x] I like homebrew content and welcome your ideas and suggestions. But make sure your suggestions support the enjoyment of the whole group.
- [x] Alignment is a thing, but I don’t pay a whole lot of attention to it. I see it more as background color for your character and as broad guidance for me on how to play NPCs. Otherwise it isn’ likely to play a significant role.
- [x] Encumbrance is NOT a thing that I particularly care to track. If a specific object or situation requires it, i will let you know.
- [x] Critical failures are NOT a thing. While a natural 1 will usually mean failing the roll, it is not a worse failure. I will, however, take advantage of natural 1 rolls to enhance the narration or raise the stakes slightly. But you do not need to worry about hitting your ally by accident or nailing your foot to the floor.
- [x] This will be much more open-ended than the previous campaign. You will often have lots of options of what to do or where to go, and I will be giving you the freedom to do so. Of course there will be in-game pressures that may influence your choices and the consequences, but I will try hard not to limit you to a single path.
- [x] Advancement will be via milestones. I anticipate players making it to at least level 16 or 17 (if not higher) by the end of the campaign. Leveling will be a bit more frequent than the last game–roughly one level every 3-5 game sessions.
- [x] Spellcasters must have the components needed for their spells when that component has a cost and is consumed by the spell. The item must be in your inventory. I will not routinely allow you to retcon and say, “Oh, I would have bought one of those at our last port.” Therefore: plan ahead!

## Rules needing group input:

- [x] What do we do in the event of a TPK (Total Party Killed)?
	- [ ] Decided in the moment depending on the situation
- [x] Will resurrections be available (at a high cost) for low level characters? If so, where and how? Will the remaining party be required to complete some kind of short quest to obtain a rare item/component needed for the resurrection?
	- [ ] Ask players what would you like to do?
- [x] In this scenario there is the potential for characters to end up with opposing goals or have other conflicts. We will discuss during Session Zero how we want to handle this (i.e. how far are we going to take the roleplay, can characters attack each other, etc.).
	- [ ] No one is opposed to PvP.
- [x] How strict we will be about using metagame knowledge during play? Generally, there are times when you as the player will know things (such as die rolls or monster abilities) that your character would not. As DM my preference is that you act whenever possible based only on your character’s in-game knowledge, but some metagaming is tolerable as long as it remains fair and fun. What are your player thoughts and concerns about this?
- [x] Will we allow metagame discussion during combat?
- [x] What do we do when someone can’t make a session?
	- [ ] Play with one or two missing
	- [ ] Other players can control the character (but perhaps with DM running the token)
- [x] Do you want to make time for “downtime” activities such as research, training, building a home, etc.? If so do you want to RP those activities, or let them happen off-stage in between sessions?
	- [ ] RP some downtime (shopping, etc.)
	- [ ] Some downtime happens in a "montage" of ability rolls (offstage or onstage)
- [x] Where on the serious/silly spectrum do we want to land? The group asked for epic fantasy, humor, horror, and dark fantasy, so there will be a mixture of things. What tone do we want most of our sessions to have?
	- [ ] Weird, fantasy, epic with humor/silly sprinkled in
- [x] DM pet peeves:
	- [ ] Players who act out-of-character to an extreme or who repeatedly disrupt the group storytelling
	- [ ] Players who roam their tokens around all over the map without waiting for the group or letting me know what you’re doing. I will assume your character is wandering off on their own and will act accordingly.
- [x] What are your game pet peeves that we should be aware of?
	- [ ] RP with complete silence--be clear when there's wait time
	- [ ] Softballs to the players
	- [ ] Guys running other people's characters (telling them what to do)
- [x] What other things have come up in game that you want to discuss with the group and the DM?

## Other topics for discussion:

- [x] Disruptive topics: are there any topics that are sensitive or concerning that you would like to limit or avoid entirely during play? If we allow certain topics, how explicit do we want them to be? Do they happen on stage or off stage?
- [x] How explicit do we want our game to be? PG, PG-13, R?
- [x] Player discomfort: We are here to have fun and play a game. If anything that happens in-game causes discomfort, how do we want to handle it? 
- [x] Rules debates: what will be our procedure if a player disagrees with a DM rule call?
- [x] Spotlight sharing: There will be times in this campaign where the story focus revolves around one character instead of the whole party. The DM will “share the wealth” and give each character their spotlight at various times. How do we want to handle it if another player does or says something in game that tramples on your fun?
- [x] Player agency: generally players can control their characters fully and take any actions they choose, but there will be in-game consequences for those choices. Murder-hobos, for example, will draw the attention of law enforcement or the wrath of other factions.
- [ ] Party splitting:
	- [ ] What is everyone’s comfort level with this?
	- [ ] How much do we want to break up the group?
	- [ ] What about characters going off on their own to do things solo? What will the rest of the group do in those situations?
	- [ ] Related to metagaming, do we all want to observe everything when the party is split, or would you rather have things be secret/private so you can RP?
