# Session: The Herdsman of Gnurlsotten's Nosh & Nip

**Purpose:** Decompression after the Monastery arc. Low stakes, high charm, one nonlethal brawl as the climax. Ends by handing the party a reason to go to [[Deastok]], and from there [[Zadash]].
**Party:** Drawg, Hesterian, Popcorn, Qilynn, Seraphina (~level 8-9)
**Reference:** [[Grent Gnurlsotten]] · [[Gnurlsotten's Nosh & Nip]]

---

## Table Card

*Everything you might need cold. Don't collapse this.*

### Grent, in three lines

- Short goliath, mistaken for a dwarf until you notice he's hairless. Grey-blue mottled skin. Stands up and the illusion breaks.
- **Gruff, fair, prickly about respect.** Cares about his goats and his cheese, not his coin.
- **Voice:** blunt, declarative, no flourishes. Calls people "boy," "little one," "friend" once he likes you. Gets genuinely annoyed if asked about his nickname — he doesn't have one.

### The three goats (escaped, now imprinted on Popcorn)

| Goat | Note |
|---|---|
| **Biscuit** | Grent's original goat. Arrived first, curled up next to Popcorn in the night. |
| **Popcorn** | Yes, same name as the PC. Grent's first line to the party is about *this* goat. |
| **Pretzel** | Arrived last, jogging, refusing to be left out. |

> Seven more in the herd — see [[Gnurlsotten's Nosh & Nip]] if a player asks.

### The regulars

| Name | Nickname | Block | Who they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yssa Marrenthal** | "Duchess" | Orc | Weathered goatherd, forty-ish, sage-grey skin, badly self-cut hair, smells of livestock. Short declarative bursts, never softens anything. **Intervenes in Beat 1.** |
| **Ferdinand Coelho** | "Lefty" | Human | Lanky, mid-thirties, always mid-sentence and losing the thread. Pats his pockets constantly. Delighted by everything. |
| **Old Ren Pallick** | "Winters" | Dwarf | Gaunt, seventy-plus, hands like knotted rope, wind-burned. Nearest the fire, same four stories in rotation. |
| **Bram Ostwick** | "Loud Bram" | Halfling | Four feet tall, thirty, gap-toothed. Voice carries through a closed door. Runs the betting in Beat 4. |

**Stat blocks:** Yssa = Scout · Ferdinand = Commoner · Old Ren = Commoner *(or Scout if the six-winters boast was true)* · Bram = Commoner

**Grent:** Gladiator — no spear, no shield, all grapples and shoves, roughly double HP. Crowd calls the fight, not zero HP. Full note in [[Grent Gnurlsotten]].

### Hollowfrost Wheel

- **80 gp a wheel** here. Resells elsewhere for roughly double. Grent knows and doesn't care.
- **Echo Tongue:** for 1 hour after eating a portion, at will you can boom your voice 3× louder for 1 minute — audible at great distance over wind, crowds, or storm. This is the "manipulate your voice" option of *thaumaturgy* and nothing else. No combat application.

### Standing rulings

> [!important] Read these before you start
> - **Two Popcorns.** After the reveal, Grent says "Popcorn" for the goat and **"little one"** for the PC. Establish this out loud at the table the first time it's confusing.
> - **The trial can't be bypassed.** Truth magic, zone of truth, a natural 20 Persuasion — none of it skips this. Grent accused a stranger loudly in front of his regulars; proving him wrong makes him *more* committed, not less, and the regulars push the trial as the face-saving ritual. Let the party prove Popcorn innocent. It changes the mood in the room, not the sequence.
> - **The brawl is nonlethal.** All damage. State it out loud before initiative.
> - **Nobody fails this session.** Every stage ends in the party being welcomed. Performance changes the story they tell afterward, not the outcome.

## Beat 0 — Three Goats on the Road

**Trigger:** On the trail through the Cyrios Mountains, before the tavern.

**What happens:** Three goats attach themselves to Popcorn, one at a time. Pacing only — play it loose.

1. **Biscuit** turns up overnight. Popcorn wakes to find it curled against him, already utterly attached.
2. **Popcorn (the goat)** catches up on the trail not long after. Same behavior, instantly.
3. **Pretzel** arrives last, practically jogging to close the distance, like it heard the others got there first.

**Outcomes:** The party will theorize — Druidic nature, Animal Handling, "goats just like him." **Never confirm which, if any, is the real reason.**

**→ Next:** Following the goats, or just continuing on, brings them to the tavern.

> **Trim:** All three goats arrive at once, overnight. One beat instead of three.
> **Stretch:** Space the arrivals across a full travel day with weather and terrain between them.

## Beat 1 — Arrival & the Accusation

**Trigger:** Party reaches [[Gnurlsotten's Nosh & Nip]] with three goats in tow.

> [!quote] Read-aloud 1 — from the road
> Ahead of you as dusk is falling, you see the warm glow of firelight lighting the trees and snow. The trail bends around a shoulder of grey rock, and there it is: a low building of mountain logs set hard against the hillside, snow banked up along its windward wall. Whoever built it knew what they were doing.
>
> Smoke rises steady from the chimney. You can hear voices inside, more than you'd expect for a building this size, this far from anywhere.
>
> A sign hangs over the door: **GNURLSOTTEN'S NOSH & NIP**. Below it, on a second board, in different lettering, clearly added years later, are the words **"...and Cheesery."**
>
> There's a goat pen off to the side. It is conspicuously, obviously under-occupied.

*(The three goats will make straight for the pen, then think better of it and come back to Popcorn.)*

> [!quote] Read-aloud 2 — the room
> Inside, it's one room. Low ceiling, four round tables, chairs worn smooth and pale where a thousand people have sat. A fire going hard in a stone hearth at the far end. It smells like woodsmoke, spilled ale, and — underneath all of it, cold and sharp and strange — something mineral, like the inside of a cave.
>
> The back wall isn't wall. It's rock. The building has been built *onto* the mountain.
>
> Four regulars are scattered around the place and none of them look up for more than a second. An orc woman sits alone with her boots up, grey-skinned and sun-lined, hair cut short and badly, watching the door with no expression whatsoever. A lanky man is halfway through a story to nobody in particular, patting his own coat pockets as he talks, increasingly urgently. Nearest the fire, an ancient dwarf — thin as rope, which dwarves generally aren't — cradles a mug and says something to the man beside him, who has plainly heard it before. And at the center table, standing on a bench so he can see over everyone, a halfling is holding forth at a volume that would carry through a closed door, a scatter of coins and a slate in front of him.
>
> Wheels of pale cheese are stacked along one wall, wrapped in cloth. Here and there, among the plain furniture and honest clutter, sit a handful of objects that very much do not belong in a roadside tavern: a lacquered box, a silver lamp, a rug worth more than the building.

> [!note]- Who's who — DM only
> Orc woman = **Yssa "Duchess" Marrenthal** · Lanky man = **Ferdinand "Lefty" Coelho** · Dwarf = **Old Ren "Winters" Pallick** · Halfling on the bench = **Bram "Loud Bram" Ostwick**
>
> Nobody is named yet. Let the party ask. The nickname thread starts the moment they do.
>
> The rock at the back is the cheese cave. **Grent will not let anyone near it.** The expensive objects are unexplained and stay that way.

> [!quote] Read-aloud 3 — the barkeep
> Behind the bar stands a stout, broad-shouldered dwarf — short, thick-built, comfortable in worn leather and flannel, hands like they've hauled a thousand kegs. He's not like any dwarf you've ever seen, though. His head is completely bare — no beard, no stubble, not even eyebrows. His skin carries a pale grey-blue tone, mottled here and there with faint darker patches, like weathered stone.
>
> He glances up as the door creaks open, giving you all a quick once-over the way anyone sizing up newcomers would. Then his eyes lock onto the shortest member of your group, and his whole face lights up.
>
> "Popcorn! You're back! I thought you were gone forever!"

**Then the confusion lands.** A genuine beat where he registers two Popcorns in the room:

> "Popcorn! You're back!" *(beat — looking at the party, not the goat)* "...wait. **Popcorn?**"

**Then the accusation:**

> "So you're the one who stole my goats! You have balls showing up here, I'll tell you that."

**What's actually going on:** His anger is real but it's bluster on top of genuine care for the goats — his hobby, not his business — and embarrassment at having jumped to a conclusion in front of his whole room. He is *not* thinking clearly. That's the character note this beat exists to deliver.

### The Goat Whisper

Grent challenges Popcorn publicly. His logic: if Popcorn only *charmed* the goats, he can't actually command them — a lucky thief, not a real handler.

> "Fine — if they're so loyal to you, prove it. Make them do something. Let's see how much they *actually* listen to you."

**Task:** Get one goat to do one specific thing — sit, come, follow, stop eating someone's boot. **Three attempts.** Player picks an approach each time.

| Approach | Check |
|---|---|
| Animal Handling | DC 12 |
| Talk to it like a person | Performance DC 12 |
| Bribe with food | DC 10, but the goat follows the *food*, not the command — counts as partial at best |
| Physical guidance | DC 15, and the goats resent it |

> [!important] Unwinnable by design — and the crowd is the scoreboard
> Grent twists both outcomes. That's the point: he's in his own head and can't process anything logically right now.
>
> **On success:** "See? I *knew* it. That's exactly how you did it, isn't it. They love you. You turned them all against me!"
> **On failure:** "See, I knew you didn't care about them. I knew you were just in it to get my goat. What do you have against cheesemongers, anyway?"
>
> **But the room reacts.** On a good roll, the regulars laugh — *at Grent*. Someone mutters. Bram says something too loud. Popcorn's success registers with everyone in the tavern except the one man it's aimed at. Play those reactions visibly; the player should see their roll land somewhere even as Grent digs in.

### Yssa intervenes

Grent has now cornered himself in front of everyone. **Yssa Marrenthal ("Duchess")** steps in — not to talk him down, but to escalate into something bigger and more public, so he can save face by doubling down instead of backing off.

She frames it as **tradition**: Grent's long-standing way of testing strangers and settling disputes. The regulars immediately confirm it, because it's true.

> "Alright, alright — if you don't believe the goats, let's see if the rest of them can pull their weight too. Make it a proper trial."

*Alternative: use Old Ren "Winters" if you want elder-authority rather than goat-authority framing.*

**→ Next:** Beat 2, the Gauntlet.

> **Trim:** One Goat Whisper attempt instead of three. Yssa intervenes immediately.
> **Stretch:** Let the party investigate the accusation — questioning regulars, tracking the goats back. They can get close to the truth (see DM Secrets) without it changing the sequence.

## Beat 2 — The Gauntlet

**Who:** Whole party. Inside the tavern and out in the snow. **Not the cheese cave** — Grent doesn't let anyone back there.

> [!important] House spirit — read this before running it
> Grent and the regulars want fun, not rules-lawyering. Each obstacle is a **goal, not a fixed check.** The listed check is a *fallback* for players who don't propose something better. Magic, teamwork, and loose interpretation are actively encouraged as long as the goal is genuinely achieved.
>
> **One constraint per obstacle**, called out by Grent or a regular right before it starts. Assigned below — don't roll for these.

### Obstacle 1 — The Wheel Roll

**Constraint:** *"Solo only — no helping hands this round."*
**Goal:** Get a full cheese wheel across the room and out the door without it toppling or crashing.
**Fallback:** Str DC 13.
**Open to:** Magic, clever use of terrain, momentum tricks.

### Obstacle 2 — The Floor is Dragon Breath

**Constraint:** *"No magic this time. Just you."*
**Goal:** Cross the crowded common room without touching the floor.
**Fallback:** Two Dex checks, summed vs. 24.
**Open to:** Teamwork — tossed and caught bench to bench, chair-stacking, route-finding across the rafters. *(Constraint deliberately blocks jump and misty step — the fun is watching them improvise physically.)*

### Obstacle 3 — The Barrel Stack

**Constraint:** *"Two of you. No more."*
**Goal:** Restack the toppled ale barrels outside before they roll downhill.
**Fallback:** Two Str checks, summed vs. 26.
**Open to:** Magic, rigging something to hold them rather than lifting, using the slope.

### Obstacle 4 — The Goat Retrieval

**Constraint:** ***Popcorn sits this one out.*** Grent insists. "Not you. Anyone but you."
**Goal:** Get Biscuit, Popcorn, and Pretzel back into the pen.
**Fallback:** DC 14 per goat, any approach.
**Open to:** Anything. Expect chaos.

> [!important] This is the callback — let it land
> The goats will not cooperate with anyone else. Not out of malice; they simply keep drifting back toward Popcorn, who is standing right there, forbidden to help.
>
> The whole tavern watches the party fail to do the one thing Popcorn did effortlessly. **Grent never concedes a word** — but everyone else in the room now understands exactly what happened, and Grent's position has quietly become indefensible.
>
> Let Popcorn eventually be allowed in. The goats come immediately. Grent says nothing.

**→ Next:** Beat 3. Yssa or Bram calls for drinks.

> **Trim:** Run Obstacle 1 and Obstacle 4 only. Obstacle 4 is the one that matters — never cut it.
> **Stretch:** Let regulars place side bets on each obstacle. Bram runs the book badly.

## Beat 3 — Wrong Answers Only

**Format:** Grent reads a question. **Any player** can pitch a wrong answer aloud. Best (worst) one — table vote or your call — gets used. The **contestant** drinks, then has to sell that answer to the crowd.

**Contestant:** One PC performs each round. Can tag out to another PC by Round 4.

| Round | Drinks | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | One | Cha DC 10 (Performance or Persuasion, player's choice) |
| **2** | Two, cumulative | Con DC 12 first — failure gives **disadvantage** on the Cha DC 12 that follows |
| **3** | Three | Con DC 14, then Cha DC 14. A regular actively tries to break their composure — heckling, a fake-out |
| **4** | All in | Con DC 16, then Cha DC 16. Another PC can step in if the contestant is fading — natural spotlight share |

## Beat 4 — The Brawl

**Trigger:** Grent steps around the bar and points at Popcorn.

> "I still don't trust you completely, little one. Prove that you're on my side. Fight me — and if you can hold your own, I'll know you're telling me the truth about the goats."

**Immediately:** tables and benches shoved aside. Bram takes bets loudly. Space cleared in seconds — this is clearly a beloved tradition, not a threat.

> [!important] Say this out loud before initiative
> In character, Grent: *"I'm not going to kill you, boy, I just want to see what you're made of."*
>
> As DM, to the table: **all damage in this fight is nonlethal.** Full weapons, spells, and abilities are fair game. Nobody drops below unconscious. There is no dying here. Real mechanical stakes, zero risk.

**This is a duel.** Grent challenged Popcorn specifically — *"prove that **you're** on my side"* — and the scene is about Popcorn earning trust in front of the room. Keep it one-on-one.

**The other four players are not idle.** Give them the crowd: placing bets with Bram, heckling, shouting terrible advice, an Insight read on whether Grent is actually pulling his punches, a Persuasion attempt to swing the room's money. Participation without initiative slots.

**Grent fights like:** a goliath who's spent thirty years breaking up bar fights. Grapples, shoves, lifts people bodily, uses furniture. Never a weapon. Never anything that looks like it's meant to seriously hurt.

**Three moves to rotate:**

1. **Grab and hoist** — lifts Popcorn off his feet and sets him down somewhere inconvenient. Crowd loves it.
2. **The shoulder** — a straight, unglamorous shove that sends him over a table.
3. **The bear hug** — grapple, squeeze, wait for him to tap or wriggle free.

**Pacing:**

- **Round 1-2:** Grent tests him. Pulls his punches visibly. **Don't drop Popcorn to 0 before the hero beat** — fudge freely.
- **Round 3:** The hero beat (below).
- **Round 4-5:** Crowd starts calling it. **Call it at Round 5** regardless, or earlier if the table's energy peaks.

> [!important] The Hero Beat — Round 3
> Grent goes for **the bear hug**. Wraps Popcorn up completely, lifts him clear off the floor. For a second it looks finished, and the room knows it.
>
> **Whatever Popcorn does to get out of it is the moment.** Escape check, spell, wild shape, bite him, a chair someone slid across the floor — it does not matter what, and it does not need to be optimal. The tavern goes dead silent, then erupts.
>
> **This is the win condition.** Not Grent's hit points. Once this lands, Grent has seen what he needed to see, and everything after is the crowd catching up. If Popcorn somehow can't break free, Grent sets him down himself, laughing, and that works too — but give the player the swing first.

> [!note]- Valve — if the duel isn't working
> **Only if** Popcorn is getting flattened, the player is visibly not enjoying it, or the fight has gone flat. Grent grins and waves the rest in:
>
> > "All of you, then! Fine!"
>
> This is a repair tool, not the default. Opening it up costs Popcorn the spotlight, so use it late and only if you need it.

**Drink carryover:** with a duel, use **Option B** from Beat 3 — Grent calls a break. *"Eat something first. I'm not fighting a drunk."* Popcorn goes in clean. *(Option A only makes sense if Popcorn was the drinking contestant and you want him fighting impaired for laughs.)*

**The close:** Grent claps Popcorn on the shoulder, or gets clapped on his own. Fully convinced. Flows straight into Beat 5.

> **Trim:** Three rounds — open, hero beat, crowd calls it.
> **Stretch:** Before the duel, Bram insists on going first as a warm-up. He is four feet tall and absolutely certain. Grent puts him on a table in one round and he could not be happier about it.

## Beat 5 — Welcome & the Favor

**Regardless of every prior outcome:** the whole tavern embraces the party. This was never a test to fail — it's a ritual for newcomers, and they've just been welcomed in. Hazing in the warmest possible sense.

Grent doesn't need to learn who really took the goats. He's more relieved than vindicated, and by now cares more about having a good story than being right.

**The prize:**
- A round on the house.
- A complimentary board of actual **Hollowfrost Wheel** — genuinely the best cheese any of them have had. It fully lives up to the legend. *(Echo Tongue applies — see Table Card.)*

### The Favor

**Marta Halsen** — the woman leading the [[Zadash]] refugees the party met earlier on the road — passed through here and left something behind. Grent knows her group won't double back, so he's sending it to the one connection he's sure of: **Torvald**, her brother-in-law at the **White Peak Inn** in [[Deastok]].

His regular cheese courier to Torvald is running late. He asks the party to carry both the delivery and the item.

> [!secret]- DM Only — what's in the package
> A **wooden comb.** Plain but well-made, clearly handmade rather than bought. Likely something Marta was bringing as a gift or keepsake for family before she had to leave in a hurry. Grent doesn't say what's inside.
>
> A note tucked in with it, in Grent's plain handwriting:
> *"Torvald — this got left behind before your sister-in-law's people moved on. Figured you'd want it more than I would. Hope things are steady down there. — G."*
>
> Nothing here connects to the rifts or the larger plot. Purely a warm, human detail.

> [!secret]- DM Only — where this goes
> The natural hook into [[Deastok]], and from there toward [[Zadash]] and the [[Korfel Withrethin]] arc. No plot pressure — a favor and an introduction.

## Running Thread — The Nicknames

*Not a gated obstacle. A social thread available all session. Pays off whenever the party pokes at it.*

Grent has **no nickname**, which is unusual for a goliath — their names are typically earned deed-names. The regulars needle newcomers with *"ask him about his nickname."* When asked:

> "Nickname? Why on Exandria would I need a nickname? Isn't my name good enough for ya? Too hard to remember? Don't want to bother?"

Genuinely, mildly annoyed. Every time.

**The four regulars' nickname origins** — deploy as color, or let players dig via asking around, buying a round, or Insight:

| Nickname | Origin |
|---|---|
| **Ferdinand "Lefty" Coelho** | Misplaced his own left boot twice in one week. Grent never let it go. *(Everyone guesses left-handed or missing a hand. No.)* |
| **Yssa "Duchess" Marrenthal** | Blunt, unglamorous goatherd. Earned it ironically after winning a card game with absurdly lucky hands. |
| **Old Ren "Winters" Pallick** | From a boast years ago that he survived six winters alone in the high passes. Sounds like a surname — easy to miss that it's a nickname at all. |
| **Bram "Loud Bram" Ostwick** | Disappointingly literal. |

**Payoff:** if the party asks Grent about his nickname *after* learning the others, the regulars will happily invent one for him on the spot. He'll be visibly annoyed. Good closing beat for Beat 5.

## DM Secrets

> [!secret]- Who actually took the goats
> Nobody stole them. A pair of young adventurers passed through days earlier, got too deep into the ale, and — convinced in their drunken state that the goats were "trapped and wanted to be free" — let them loose as a misguided act of kindness. The goats wandered until they found Popcorn.
>
> Grent doesn't know this and may never find out unless the party investigates. **He doesn't need to** for Beat 5 to land.
