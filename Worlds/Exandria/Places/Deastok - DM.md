## Description

City in the Truscan Vale, Dwendalian Empire. Sister city to [[Kamordah]]. Sits on a hill bordering the Cyrengreen Forest, surrounded by fenced, segmented farmland. Population ~10,090 (60% human, 26% dwarf, 10% halfling, 4% other). Nominally led by Starosta Deter McGinneas; real power rests with the Truscan family. Single official temple, dedicated to Bahamut. Comfortable, affluent, quietly confident rather than showy.

**Districts:**

- [[Deastok|Bursar Plaza]]—commerce and market stalls. The town's original core, historically a laborers' district (hence the temple's location), gentrified over generations into a "quaint countryside getaway" image aimed at tourists—marketing, not history.
- [[Deastok|Garden Grounds]]—old-money residential, parks and open space. Deastok's genuine affluent locals. [[White Peak Inn]] and Jorun's house are here.
- [[Deastok|Jeweled Gates]]—blue-roofed western area plus the checkpoint plaza near the watchtower. Working and merchant class, clean and well-kept. **No run-down parts of Deastok exist.**
- Farmland beyond the town—Truscan-owned; site of the survey.

**Security:** no formal walls. Checkpoints at major roads in and out, not a fortified perimeter. A confident town that doesn't need to look embattled.

> [!info] Tone calibration—the Empire here, not at the front
> Deastok is a prosperous inland Empire town, not a border posting. The party should feel the Empire is *present* and *paying attention*—propaganda, paperwork, a watchful eye—with nothing that reads as a war footing. Guards are bored more than alert. The checkpoint exists because the Empire always checkpoints.
>
> **This is the party's first exposure to an Empire-wide routine, not a Deastok quirk.** Establish it clearly here; it recurs without re-explanation.

## Places in Deastok

### Jeweled Gates: arrival

> [!quote]- Boxed text: approaching the town
> "The road climbs a last low rise, and the watchtower comes up first—a lone stone finger against the sky, a thin curl of smoke rising from a brazier at its top. Only as the road bends does the rest of the town appear below it: rooftops, smoke from a hundred chimneys, the glint of a distant fountain, and beyond the far edge of the fields, the dark line of the forest."

**Entry checklist (mix and match, don't force every beat):**

- [ ] The watchtower is visible well before anything else. Establish it first.
- [ ] The road narrows toward a checkpoint; a short line of carts and travelers waits, unhurried.
- [ ] **Papers, every time, every town.** Two questions at every checkpoint from here on: *where are you coming from* (last stop, not a full itinerary) and *what's your business* (broad categories). Both recorded with name, race, and brief description onto a running travel log—each town's clerk adds an entry rather than issuing fresh papers.
- [ ] Anyone without papers is sent to a clerk who draws up a set on the spot. Flat fee **1 sp per person.** Mild friction, nothing more.
- [ ] **Nothing is verified.** No answer is checked against anything. Paperwork as normalized habit, not active security—make the distinction felt.
- [ ] Guards visibly going through the motions. A bored or resentful comment about the posting is good humanizing color, not a hook.
- [ ] **If asked for directions:** dry and short first—"Do I look like a travel agent?"—then a beat, then softer: "Forgive me. I was supposed to go on leave two weeks ago and my replacement hasn't shown. I suppose they needed him in the Ashkeeper Peaks." Directions follow, no further complaint.
- [ ] Notice board: tax notices, lost livestock, a festival date, a conscription notice worded administratively rather than urgently.
- [ ] Ambient chatter carries mild war-adjacent unease—supply costs, a cousin called up east—without anyone treating it as an emergency. **Deastok reads about the war; it doesn't feel it.**
- [ ] Once through, tone shifts immediately to ordinary town business. Nothing about the checkpoint lingers.

> [!quote]- Boxed text: entering through the gate
> "The road narrows before the gate, funneled between a low customs house and a scatter of wagons waiting their turn. Nobody is in a hurry. A clerk waves through a cart of barrels with barely a glance. A horse stamps and snorts, tied outside a stable that's seen better paint."

**Checkpoint Plaza features:**

- [ ] Customs/toll house—routine bureaucracy, not military inspection.
- [ ] Carters' and caravan yard.
- [ ] A modest travelers' waystation—smaller and plainer than [[White Peak Inn]] or [[Shaded Bough]].
- [ ] Public well or fountain.
- [ ] Short row of stalls catering to arrivals.
- [ ] Notice board.
- [ ] [[Deastok|Crownsguard Barracks]]—present, one building among several, not the focal point.
- [ ] **The statue**—the plaza's centerpiece.

#### The statue—Bahamut and Emperor Dwendal

Stone, weathered but well-kept. Gilded accents rather than full paint: gold leaf on the Emperor's regalia and crown, platinum on Bahamut's armor and draconic motifs. Bahamut in his paladin-warrior guise, arm-in-arm with a rendered Emperor Dwendal. Small gold-leafed canaries in flight around the pair.

> [!quote]- Boxed text: the statue
> "Two figures rise from the plaza's center in weathered stone, gilded where the light catches them—a towering armored form, wings folded, the Platinum Dragon shaped as a warrior, one gauntleted hand resting on the shoulder of a crowned man half his height. Small gold birds circle the pair in flight, one settled on the dragon's pauldron."

### The Bahamut Temple

The temple keeps the Empire at its threshold, not its heart. A small foyer nods to civic piety; past it, the sanctuary belongs entirely to the Platinum Dragon.

> [!quote]- Boxed text: entering the sanctuary
> "A carved relief in the foyer gives thanks for the Emperor's blessing over the city. Beyond the inner archway, platinum light falls through high windows onto a wide, quiet sanctuary. Frescoes wrap the walls in unbroken procession—dragons in flight, in battle, in council, no two alike. Gold scales catch the light again and again, but not only gold: a hammered bronze form grapples something clawed and shadowed near the eastern wall; a curl of silver watches over a council of mortals from the shadow of a painted mountain; brass wings spread wide above a battlefield strewn with banners; a deep warm copper shape rests coiled around a sleeping village."

### Bursar Plaza

Central market and gathering space. The town's original core, gentrified into a countryside-getaway image for tourists and merchants. Dr. Wenna Alsott's practice is here. [[Shaded Bough]] sits on or just off the plaza.

> [!quote]- Boxed text: entering from the northwest
> "The street opens all at once into an enormous plaza, broad enough that the buildings on the far side look small. A great fountain anchors the center. Around it, three separate clusters of tents and stalls spread across the open stone—awnings in every color, the noise of bartering carrying across the space. To the south, rising above the rooftops, a dome catches the sun—pale, faintly luminous, unmistakably platinum."

**Entry checklist:**

- [ ] Establish the scale first. This is the town's largest open space, not a side-street market.
- [ ] Three distinct stall clusters—good for splitting up or wandering.
- [ ] The temple dome is visible, marking its direction without a separate discovery.
- [ ] Ambient life: haggling, a busker, someone selling the "authentic Deastok countryside" experience to a visibly touristy couple.
- [ ] [[Shaded Bough]] visible if useful.
- [ ] **The fruit-stall hook lands here.** See Scene 1.

### Garden Grounds

Old-money residential, parks, red-roofed on the working map. Deastok's genuine affluent locals—the quiet unperforming wealth the [[Shaded Bough]]'s tourist crowd assumes it has found. Jorun's house is here.

### White Peak Inn

Torvald's inn, in [[Deastok|Garden Grounds]] among the old-money residents it actually serves. A small sign on the door, no advertising. Known by the people who already know it's there. Torvald is Henrik Halsen's brother—Henrik being Marta's husband—which is why the comb went to him directly. [[Bramwell Bellweather]] is a known fixture.

> [!info] DM reminder—Torvald's tone
> Baseline: courteous but uninvested. Paying customers get good service and nothing more. **The shift to warmth is triggered specifically by Marta's name or the comb**, not by anything else the party does. Once warm, he raises Jorun himself—see Scene 1.

> [!quote]- Boxed text: entering
> "A small sign on the door reads only WHITE PEAK INN, no flourish, nothing visible from more than a few steps away. Inside the room is warm and unhurried. Torvald stands behind the bar—a broad, weathered human well into his fifties, sleeves rolled past thick forearms, a neatly kept grey beard, wiping a glass he finished drying some time ago. A few regulars sit without looking up: an old woman working through a bowl of stew one slow bite at a time; two dwarven men in dusty work clothes trading a low joke over half-empty tankards; a younger woman by the window reading, a small dog asleep against her boot; a stout, formally dressed man near the fire, alone, a drink in front of him he hasn't touched in a while."

**First visit:**

- [x] Torvald greets them plainly, seats them, takes an order. No questions about where they're from.
- [x] The room is quiet and local. No performance, nothing aimed at a visitor.
- [ ] **Comb or Marta's name → the shift.** Warmer, more present. May sit with them, asks after Marta and Henrik by name. **Then raises Jorun.**
- [ ] Without the comb: nothing happens. Pleasant and unremarkable until it does.

**Bramwell, present from the first visit:**

- [ ] Already in the room description—the stout, formally dressed man near the fire. No separate introduction. If asked about him: "Bramwell? Fixture, that one. Kamordah family, or was. Harmless."
- [ ] **(Optional)** He can be overheard grumbling to Torvald about correspondence—"another letter, always wanting more, as if I've nothing better to do"—establishing a tedious obligation with zero specifics.

### Shaded Bough

The flashy tourist-facing inn, on or just off [[Deastok|Bursar Plaza]] for maximum visibility. Real Deastok gentry avoid it.

> [!quote]- Boxed text: passing or entering
> "A carved wooden sign in gilt lettering swings above the door: THE SHADED BOUGH—DEASTOK'S FINEST. A doorman in a crisp green coat greets everyone who approaches as 'my lord' or 'my lady' without asking. Inside, the common room is exposed beam and hanging lantern, a lute playing somewhere, every table full."

### The Dig Site

Truscan-owned farmland northeast of town, a short walk or ride past the [[Deastok|Jeweled Gates]] checkpoint. Windmills stand around it, and a hole with a 30-foot drop lies past the exposed stone. See Scene 2. **Unnamed—name it only if it recurs.** No second Truscan household near it; simply Truscan fields, unconnected to any manor building.

### Truscan Estate (Manor)

The large walled estate in the southwest near the forest edge—the actual seat of the Truscan family, separate from the northeastern dig site. See Scene 2 for Aldous Penwrye and the permission scene.
