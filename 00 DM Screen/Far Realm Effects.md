| 1‑2 | Recursive Memory | One random creature suddenly remembers dying.<br><br>"You see a figure lying on the ground. Describe where they are and what's around them." Let them build the scene.<br><br>"The figure is dead—who or what killed them?"<br><br>Then: "As you look closer at the figure's face, you recognize it. It's you. And now you *remember* this happening. You remember dying. You remember every detail of it."<br><br>The memory is vivid, detailed, and feels as real as any other memory. It doesn't fade. *Greater restoration* suppresses the memory for 1d4 weeks; only *wish* removes it entirely.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| 3   | The Passenger    | Over multiple days, one random creature slowly becomes aware of a second presence in their mind.<br><br>First occurrence: "An unexpected thought crosses your mind. What is it?"<br><br>Later: "That same thought again, but now it has an opinion about what you're doing. What does it think?"<br><br>Eventually: "You realize the thought isn't *yours*. It's watching through you. What does it want to see?"<br><br>The affected creature has disadvantage on saving throws against divination spells and effects, as the passenger makes their mind easier to find. The presence can only be expelled by *dispel evil and good* upcast using a 7th-level slot or higher, and when it leaves, the creature must succeed on a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw or feel profound, inexplicable grief at its departure.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 4‑5 | The Recognized   | One random creature notices a stranger and becomes certain they know them. Ask the player: "Someone catches your eye. A stranger—describe them briefly." Then: "You're absolutely certain you know them. Where do you know them from?"<br><br>The stranger has no idea who the character is. This happens again with a completely different person—different race, gender, age. But the character's memory rewrites itself: "Of course. This is who they always were. That memory of an elf? No, you must have been tired. Obviously they were a half-orc." <br><br>Each new version feels *more* true than the last. The character can never trust when their memory "clarifies" something—is it real clarity, or is something rewriting them? *Greater restoration* suppresses the effect for 1d4 weeks. Only *wish* ends it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| 6   | The Debt         | One random creature is now marked. Nothing happens immediately. During a moment of crisis or near-death: "Something intervenes. You feel a presence offer you a way out—not words, just certainty. What would you sacrifice to survive this moment?"<br><br>If they hesitate, offer gentle nudges: "A memory. A person. A place that feels like home. A future you imagined. A promise you made."<br><br>If they offer something trivial, the presence passes through the offered thing without slowing, like it doesn't exist in whatever economy this bargain operates in. The dying continues. The presence is still waiting—and now they understand: it wants something that would *hurt* to lose.<br><br>Whatever they name—a memory, a relationship, a future possibility—accept it. They survive through improbable luck. Then, 1d4 days later: "You try to recall [the thing they named], but it's… blurry. Fading. Describe what you're losing." The named thing erodes over the following week until it's gone entirely. A forgotten person doesn't remember them either. A sacrificed future possibility becomes impossible. Only *wish* can restore what was given. |
| 7‑8 | The Promise      | One creature hears a voice—not in their ears, in their *understanding*—ask a simple question. The voice feels curious, almost warm. "What will you protect?" You want to answer. What do you say?<br><br>Whatever they answer, the creature immediately knows that this thing is now *known* to something else. You feel seen. You feel understood.<br><br>Going forward, whenever that thing is threatened, the creature feels a pulse of attention—not menacing, just *present*. Something is watching. Something cares about the outcome. <br><br>There is no resolution to this effect. No research reveals anything useful. No NPC can explain it. The promise simply *is*. When Ceratos is defeated (or fully manifests), the attention stops without explanation—the character was never in a bargain, never being tested. Ceratos simply noticed them the way one might notice an insect. The "promise" was meaning the character assigned to something that had none. The player learns this through the Ceratos revelation; the character may never fully understand.                                                                                                   |
